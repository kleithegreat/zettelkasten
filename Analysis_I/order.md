# Ordering of Rational Numbers
**Definition:** Let $a$ and $b$ be two [[rational_number|rational]] (or [[real_number|real]]) numbers. We say that $x > y$ iff $x - y$ is a positive number, and $x < y$ iff $x - y$ is a negative number.

We weite $x \geq y$ to mean that either $x > y$ or $x = y$, and similarly for $x \leq y$.

Basic properties of this ordering include:
1. **Trichotomy:** Exactly one of the following is true: $x < y$, $x = y$, or $x > y$.
2. **Anti-symmetry:** $x < y$ iff $y > x$.
3. **Transitivity:** If $x < y$ and $y < z$, then $x < z$.
4. **Addition preserves order:** If $x < y$, then for any $z$, $x + z < y + z$.
5. **Multiplication preserves order:** If $x < y$ and $z > 0$, then $x \cdot z < y \cdot z$.
